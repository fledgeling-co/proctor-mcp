CASE-0115 · aVerificationInFlightBlocksNothing
Added after the first fix was measured and found to have reshaped the wedge rather than
removed it. Arming: the blocking single-flight put back — waiters on an NSCondition, the
verification on the first caller's own thread, which is what the first fix did.

Note that the Swift compiler refuses that shape outright once verdict(for:) is async:

  error: instance method 'lock' is unavailable from asynchronous contexts;
         Use async-safe scoped locking instead

so the arming had to route the blocking through a synchronous helper — which is exactly how
the original cache escaped the check, being a synchronous function called from an async one.

Red:
  Test "a verification in flight holds no cooperative thread, so the rest of the process runs"
  recorded an issue at ToolchainDoctorTests.swift:419:9: Expectation failed:
  (finishedBeforeTheVerificationLanded.current → 0) == (unrelated → 32)
  failed after 20.001 seconds with 1 issue.

Zero of thirty-two unrelated tasks ran in twenty seconds while seventeen callers waited on one
verification. That is the wedge, in a unit test, deterministic.

Green after restoring the async version, with the whole suite:

  Test run with 8 tests in 1 suite passed after 0.391 seconds.

CASE-0116 · theVerificationRunsOffTheCooperativePool
Added 2026-08-21 after a fresh verifier measured what CASE-0115 does not discriminate: he
replaced the cache's dedicated `Thread` with a `Task.detached` routed through a synchronous
helper — the verification straight back on the cooperative pool — and BOTH concurrency tests
stayed green. CASE-0110 and CASE-0115 measure the waiters, who suspend either way.

This case reads the root queue libdispatch reports for each half, so the execution context is
measured rather than inferred from timing. Measured on this machine, 2026-08-21:

    Thread                   com.apple.root.default-qos.overcommit
    Task.detached            com.apple.root.default-qos.cooperative
    DispatchQueue.global()   com.apple.root.default-qos

Arming 1 — the verifier's own substitution, `Task.detached` through a synchronous helper:

  Test "the verification runs off the cooperative pool, on a thread the pool cannot refuse"
  recorded an issue at ToolchainDoctorTests.swift:485:9: Expectation failed:
  !((verificationRanOn.current → "com.apple.root.default-qos.cooperative")
    .contains("cooperative") → true)
  ... and at :487:9 against .contains("overcommit").
  failed after 0.001 seconds with 2 issues.

In that same run, "fifteen callers arriving together on a cold entry cost one verification"
and "a verification in flight holds no cooperative thread" both passed — the verifier's
finding, reproduced here rather than taken on trust.

Arming 2 — `DispatchQueue.global().async`, the shape that looks like the fix and is starved by
the same cap (grok-4.6 named it during the design review as a substitution that reads as B and
is not):

  Expectation failed: (verificationRanOn.current → "com.apple.root.default-qos")
  .contains("overcommit")
  failed after 0.001 seconds with 1 issue.

Green after restoring the shipped `Thread`, whole suite:

  Test run with 9 tests in 1 suite passed after 0.084 seconds.

What it does NOT measure: that a verification completes while every cooperative thread is
blocked. That was drafted as a saturation test and dropped deliberately — it holds the whole
process-wide pool for seconds in a green run, and the suite runs its tests in parallel, so it
would stall unrelated tests and feed DEF-051's 0.2 s bound. The queue label is the same claim
measured at the mechanism instead of through a timing race.
