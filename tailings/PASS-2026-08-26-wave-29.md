## Finding 1 — Three of four accepted sockets can still be killed by SIGPIPE

`DEF-338` is recorded `fixed`. The fix reaches one of the four places the process writes
to a peer it did not open.

`Server.swift:108` states the rule the fix rests on: *"An ACCEPTED descriptor does not
inherit SO_NOSIGPIPE from its"* listener. `Server.swift` applies `proctorSuppressSIGPIPE`
to both its listener (`:43`) and its accepted client (`:112`). The other three apply it to
neither the accepted descriptor nor, in one case, the listener:

| File | Listener | Accepted client | Writes to the client |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Sources/ProctorAgent/Server.swift` | `:43` | `:112` | — |
| `Sources/ProctorReflector/SocketServer.swift` | `:47` | **absent** | `writeAll` at `:123`, `:128`, `:146` |
| `Sources/ProctorAgent/Unlock/UnlockBroker.swift` | `:102` | **absent** | `send(client, …, 0)` at `:137`, `:148` |
| `Sources/ProctorShim/RemoteServer.swift` | **absent** | **absent** | `write(client, …)` at `:288` |

`UnlockBroker` passes flags `0` to `send` rather than `MSG_NOSIGNAL`, so nothing suppresses
the signal on that path either.

Evidence: `grep -n "socket(AF_\|accept(\|proctorSuppressSIGPIPE\|SO_NOSIGPIPE"` over the four
files, and `grep -n "write(\|send(\|writeAll"` over the three unsuppressed ones. Both are
reproduced above by line number.

Confidence: high that the suppression is absent, because the greps are exhaustive over those
files. Not measured that the signal fires — DEF-338's original reproduction (exit 141 on a
write to a closed peer) has not been re-run against these three sockets, and it needs a
second write after the FIN has landed rather than one.

`ProctorReflector` is the worst of the three: it is embedded in somebody else's application,
so the process a SIGPIPE terminates is the host's.

Recommendation: a defect and a brief, not a fix from this pass. Editing product code is
outside what a tailings pass has established.
