## Finding 1 — proctor-shim's remote transport could be killed by SIGPIPE, and the rule that hid it was never measured

**Corrected after the pass, by measuring the premise.** The finding as first written said
three of four socket servers were defective. One was. The correction is recorded here rather
than rewritten away, because the mechanism that produced the wrong count is the more useful
half.

`Server.swift:108` carried a comment asserting kernel behaviour: *"An ACCEPTED descriptor does
not inherit SO_NOSIGPIPE from its listener"*. Reading the tree against it gave four bare
descriptors across three files. Measured on Darwin 25.6.0, with the listener suppressed and
bare, on both families:

| family | listener suppressed | listener reports | accepted reports |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `AF_UNIX` | no | 0 | 0 |
| `AF_UNIX` | yes | 1 | **1** |
| `AF_INET` | no | 0 | 0 |
| `AF_INET` | yes | 1 | **1** |

The accepted descriptor inherits, and inherits the absence too. So
`Sources/ProctorReflector/SocketServer.swift` and `Sources/ProctorAgent/Unlock/UnlockBroker.swift` suppressed
on their listeners and their accepted clients were covered; neither was ever defective.

`Sources/ProctorShim/RemoteServer.swift` was. It suppressed on neither its `AF_INET` listener (`:69`)
nor the descriptor it accepted (`:94`), and writes the HTTP reply down that descriptor at
`:288`. Reproduced in a child process at **exit 141** on both families, and at **exit 0** with
`EPIPE` or `ECONNRESET` once the listener is suppressed —
`scripts/campaign/sigpipe_disposition_probe.py`, driven from
`Tests/ProctorCoreTests/AcceptedSocketSignalTests.swift`.

Confidence: high on both halves, because both are measurements rather than readings. The first
version of this finding was a reading, and it was wrong by two.

Recorded as DEF-342 (the reachable fault, fixed) and DEF-343 (the comment, corrected). The
census that makes it non-recurring is `scripts/campaign/socket_signal_census.py`: it fires on
the pre-fix tree at `bf34a985` naming exactly those four descriptors, and passes at 9 of 9 now.
